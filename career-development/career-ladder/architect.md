# Software Architect

The architect owns the design that more than one team builds on.

*This page is an outline. The sections will be filled in over time, including a C# sample where the topic has one.*

## What the role owns

The boundaries, the shared foundations, and the decisions other teams are expected to follow.

## Writing the foundation code

The architect writes the first version of the shared code so a pattern exists before other features copy it.

## Reviewing critical changes

The architect reviews a change that crosses a boundary, changes a contract, or would be hard to undo.

## Explaining the design to several teams

The same design is explained in each team's terms, until that team can apply it without another meeting.

## Trade-offs and tech debt

Each design names what it gives up, and which debt is accepted on purpose versus debt that has no owner.

## Recording decisions

The choice is written where the teams can find it, in the form described in [Decision Making](/career-development/team-operations/decision-making).

## Production incidents

The architect joins an incident that comes from the design, and carries what broke into the next change to that design.

## Keeping the stack current

The architect tracks which parts of the stack are falling behind, and schedules the upgrades the product depends on.

## Mentoring

The architect reviews designs with engineers who are ready to own a boundary, and lets them make the next decision.

## Using AI suggestions without handing over judgment

An AI suggestion is a draft. The architect still chooses the option, the trade-off, and whether the change ships.

## Moving from senior IC to architect

The move is from owning a feature to owning decisions several teams share, while still writing the foundation those teams use.
